At the intersection of generative modeling and the natural sciences lie questions
of two kinds: what can such models, in principle, represent and learn, and how
can they be brought to bear on the problems scientists actually face? We pursue
both.

On the theoretical side, we study the mathematical foundations of generative
models — their expressivity, sampling guarantees, and the geometry of the
distributions they learn. On the applied side, we develop methodologies tailored
to scientific data, with current efforts spanning molecular and protein structure
generation, sampling from physical systems, and scientific data augmentation.
